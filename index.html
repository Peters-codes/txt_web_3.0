Chapter Twelve
The Mirror of Secrets

The corridors of the old castle were drafty and cold that evening. Young Alistair pulled his robes tighter as he crept past the portraits, their painted eyes seeming to follow his every move. He wasn't supposed to be out after curfew, but the map in his pocket glowed with a faint blue light, urging him onward.

He had heard whispers in the common room about a hidden room on the seventh floor — a place that appeared only to those who truly needed it. Some called it the Room of Requirement, but the older students spoke of it in hushed tones as simply "the wand room."

Alistair stopped before a blank stretch of wall between two tapestries depicting forgotten battles. He closed his eyes and paced three times, thinking desperately of a place where he could practice spells without being caught. On his third turn, a smooth door materialized from the stone.

Chapter Twelve
The Mirror of Secrets

The room inside was impossibly vast, filled with towering piles of forgotten furniture, broken instruments, and trunks left behind by generations of students. Moonlight streamed through a high window, illuminating a dusty mirror standing alone in the center of the chamber.

It was unlike any mirror he had seen before. The frame was twisted silver, and the glass seemed to swirl like fog. As Alistair stepped closer, his reflection did not mimic him. Instead, it smiled — a smile he had not made — and raised a hand to point at something behind him.

He spun around, heart pounding, but saw only shadows. When he turned back to the glass, the reflection was writing words in the condensation with its finger: "Three turns, a secret wish, and the wand chooses the wizard."

Outside, an owl hooted in the darkness. Alistair reached out and touched the cold surface of the mirror, wondering if some secrets were better left undisturbed.

Chapter Twelve
The Mirror of Secrets

Behind him, the door creaked. Alistair spun around, hand instinctively reaching for his pocket, but it was only the wind slipping through a crack in the ancient stone. Still, the hairs on the back of his neck stood on end. The mirror had shown him something — or someone — and he couldn't shake the feeling that he was no longer alone.

He backed away slowly, never taking his eyes off the swirling glass. The reflection waved goodbye, its grin widening into something that made Alistair's blood run cold. He turned and ran, robes billowing behind him, the sound of soft laughter echoing through the piles of forgotten things.

By the time he reached the common room, breathless and pale, his housemates were already asleep. He climbed into bed and pulled the curtains shut, but sleep did not come easily. Somewhere in the castle, the mirror waited — and it knew his name.
